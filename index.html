Our story 💛

Born and raised in Altona, run with heart — meet the family behind the centre.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Grace & Tom — our owners

Altona Early Education Centre is owned by Grace and Tom Azzopardi, a local family born and raised right here in Altona.

Grace worked in Altona as a registered psychologist before opening the centre, spent years volunteering at the Royal Children's Hospital supporting preschoolers with developmental challenges, and is a qualified kindergarten teacher. Tom works in road construction and is often found hands-on at the centre — when he's not busy raising their four children!

⭐ Sarah — our centre director

Sarah brings 21 years of experience in early education and is just as passionate today as the day she started. She's here five days a week, wearing many hats as mentor and leader.

Families love her friendly nature, attention to detail and wonderful communication. Feel free to give Sarah a call for a chat about bookings.

Happy educators, happy kids

Grace, Tom and Sarah believe a centre thrives on passionate educators. That's why our team receives exceptional support, above-award wages, and opportunities to keep upskilling — so the children and the educators feel empowered and happy here every day.

In 2023 we grew our family of services, opening a preschool at 47 Railway St, Altona and a City Farm Kinder program at 112 Civic Pde, Altona.

Our rooms 🏡

Smaller rooms build closer relationships — our early education rooms cater for just 12 children, and our kinder rooms 22, always with extra educators and higher qualifications.

Room 1 · Babies 🍼

6 weeks – 18 months

Our youngest room, with two dedicated sleep rooms to help even the fussiest sleeper drift off, plus a big sandpit and plenty of space for those exciting first steps.

A large undercover area keeps play going, rain or shine.

  • 👶 12 children (max 8 non-mobile)
  • 🧑‍🏫 3 educators

Room 2 · Toddlers 🚂

18 months – 2.5 years

A big, bright toddler room with its own yard, a corner sandpit and a wooden stage made for dancing, singing and imaginary play.

The yard is partly undercover, so outdoor play happens every single day.

  • 👧 12 children
  • 🧑‍🏫 3 educators

Room 3 · Junior Kinder 🎨

2.5 – 3.5 years

Our younger kindergarten room, running a funded 3-year-old kinder program for 5 hours a day, fully integrated into long day care — so no child misses out.

Led by full-time kinder teacher Dianne with a team of diploma-trained co-educators.

  • 🧒 22 children
  • 🧑‍🏫 5 educators

Room 4 · School Readiness 🎒

3.5 – 6 years

Our oldest group, focused on school readiness through a funded 4-year-old kindergarten program woven into the long day care day.

Led by full-time kinder teacher Elise and her team of diploma-qualified educators.

  • 🧒 22 children
  • 🧑‍🏫 Full-time kinder teacher + team

Our kitchen 🍳

Nutritious, delicious, and made with love every day by our chef Lauren.

👩‍🍳 Meet Lauren

Lauren has been cooking in early education for over a decade, and her love for it hasn't faded a bit. She spends time with the children and families every day, and her favourite thing is broadening little tastebuds — stir-fried oyster mushrooms, fresh bean shoots, chickpeas and more.

For fussier eaters, Lauren blends extra vegetables into every meal so no child misses their daily goodness.

🥗 Our menu

Our menu is built on research and collaboration with local paediatric dietitian Melanie Bouras and Nutrition Australia, with plenty of input from our families and children.

Menus rotate on a 4-week seasonal cycle — Summer, Autumn, Winter and Spring — introducing new tastes and textures all year round.

Little chefs welcome! 🧁

Our children regularly join in the cooking fun — growing and cooking their own kale chips and pumpkin soup, baking traditional damper and orange muffins, and once even baking a special batch of cookies for our local firefighters!

Our philosophy 🌱

Every child is unique and has the right to feel appreciated and valued.

🏠

A home away from home

We build a strong foundation for each child to grow at their own pace, in an environment that's homely, clean, safe, happy and stimulating.

🤝

Families first

Our open-door policy welcomes families in. We know families are the biggest influence in a child's life, so we build our programs around your values, needs and interests.

📚

Quality frameworks

Our educational program is built on the Early Years Learning Framework (EYLF), the National Quality Framework (NQF) and national regulations, adapting to each child's needs.

🧑‍🏫

Professional educators

Every educator holds ACECQA-approved qualifications, a current Working with Children Check, first aid, CPR and anaphylaxis training, plus child protection training.

🍎

Health & nutrition

We cater for all dietary needs — allergies, religious and personal preferences — and keep every mealtime positive, relaxed and social.

✨

Growing our team

Educators keep developing professionally and share their own gifts — languages, music, creativity — to enrich every child's experience.

Child safety 🛡️

The safety, health and wellbeing of the children in our care is our number one priority.

Our commitment to child safety & wellbeing

🚫

Zero tolerance

We will not tolerate harm, the risk of harm, bullying or harassment — and we act on every concern, encouraging a culture of reporting.

👂

We listen to children

We uphold children's rights and empower them to know and exercise those rights. Children and families help make the decisions that affect them.

🌏

Inclusion for all

We're committed to equity and inclusion regardless of ability, sex, gender, or social, economic and cultural background.

🤝

Culturally welcoming

We provide an inclusive environment for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, respecting and valuing their unique identities and experiences.

The 11 Child Safe Standards

Victoria's Child Safe Standards are embedded in our daily routines and long-term planning. Tap each standard to see how we bring it to life.

1Culturally safe environments for Aboriginal children
Aboriginal children's identities, histories and rights are respected and celebrated. Educators train in cultural safety and weave Aboriginal perspectives into the curriculum, with families consulted along the way.
2Leadership, governance and culture
Child safety is built into our leadership and everyday culture. Every team member models child-safe behaviour under a clear Code of Conduct, with a designated child safety officer driving accountability.
3Child empowerment and participation
Children learn about their rights and take part in decisions that affect them. Educators listen, respect boundaries, and teach protective behaviours using child-friendly materials.
4Family and community engagement
Families are partners in safety. We communicate openly, seek feedback, obtain consent for key activities, and invite families into decision-making and cultural events.
5Equity and diversity
We respond to the diverse needs of all children — including Aboriginal, culturally diverse, LGBTQI and disability communities — with inclusive practice, diverse representation and cultural celebration.
6Suitable and supported staff
Staff, volunteers and students are carefully screened, inducted and supervised, with recruitment, reviews and professional development all reinforcing child-safe practice.
7Child-focused complaints process
Our complaints system puts children first — accessible, transparent and culturally safe. Children are taught how to raise concerns, and every complaint is taken seriously.
8Staff knowledge, skills and awareness
Ongoing training helps our team identify and respond to risks of harm, covering cultural safety, trauma-informed care and protective behaviours. Child safety is a standing agenda item in staff meetings.
9Safe physical and online environments
Clean, well-supervised spaces with strict supervision protocols — children are never left alone. Digital safety is upheld through secure device use, privacy protections and online-safety education.
10Continuous improvement
Policies are reviewed regularly against current law and best practice, informed by feedback from children, families and staff, with incidents analysed to strengthen safeguards.
11Policies and procedures
All child-safety practices are clearly documented, accessible to staff and families, reviewed annually and consistently followed by a trained team.

📋 Reportable Conduct Scheme

The Reportable Conduct Scheme, administered by the Commission for Children and Young People (CCYP), requires organisations to respond to allegations of child abuse and child-related misconduct by workers and volunteers.

The Child Safe Standards are proactive — building environments that protect children. The Reportable Conduct Scheme is reactive — making sure allegations are properly investigated and reported. Together they build a culture of safety with real accountability.
